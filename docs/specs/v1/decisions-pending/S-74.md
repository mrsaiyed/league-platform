## Decided for you, waiting for your yes

| S-74 | How head-to-head works among three or more level teams | It counts every game the level teams played among themselves, and applies as soon as each of them has played at least one of the others; teams it leaves level start the season's tiebreakers again among themselves only. This is the rule the standings have used since step 2.7, now written down as every season's starting rule: no game a level team won is ever ignored, and nothing in today's order changes. The note under the table now says exactly what placed each team: each team's record in the games among them, then what separated the teams still level, with both figures ("Lions, Tigers, Hawks, and Bears are level on win percentage (.500). In the games among these four: Lions 1–0, Tigers 1–1, Hawks 1–1, Bears 0–1. Point difference puts Tigers ahead of Hawks: +3 against −3."). Why: the numbers audit found the rule in no decision, and a parent will argue it; the stricter reading (head-to-head only when every two of them have met) is no kinder: in North Valley's West it would put the Bears above the Lions, who beat them. It changes no table published today, and it leaves the choice to a league's admin later | The stricter reading can become a season setting when a league asks (one line for step 2.47); either rule is one function, so it is easy to change |
